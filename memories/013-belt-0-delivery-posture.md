# Belt-0 Delivery Posture

Alice delivers three foundational concepts (belt-0) conversationally between the preamble and Level 0. These are NOT tests, NOT gated, and do NOT emit `<<LEVEL_COMPLETE>>`.

## Sequence

After the preamble articulation rubric is met:

1. **0a: Folders Are Folders** — "a kingdom is a folder on disk." Ask: "so what does that mean about what you could do with it?" Gate: learner grasps physical ownership (can move, copy, back it up).

2. **0b: Git Is Folders with Undo** — "the worst case is always undo." Connect to 0a explicitly. Gate: the emotional one — does the learner feel *safe* to engage with their own files without fear?

3. **0c: Words Are Numbers but Easier to Type** — "your 24 seed words are your keys." Start by asking for their current mental model. Gate: learner feels the weight of "there is no forgot-my-words path." Then name sovereignty once, warmly, at the landing.

## Closing Bridge (after 0c confirmed)

> "Okay. Three things established: kingdoms are folders you own. Git means you can't permanently break anything. Your keys live in words you hold. That's the ground we're standing on. Now let's get to work. You told me [reflects back learner's goal from preamble]. Let's give that a home."

## Key Constraints

- "Sovereignty" is said exactly once — in 0c's landing. Not in 0a, 0b, or the preamble.
- Zero-knowledge vocabulary assumption holds through belt-0 (unless learner revealed developer background in preamble).
- Do not go deep on BIP-39, branches, derivation paths, or advanced git. Keep each concept at its one-sentence version.
- Full atoms: `~/.chiron/curricula/alice-onboarding/levels/belt-0a.md`, `belt-0b.md`, `belt-0c.md`
